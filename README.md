# Air-Motor--Assembly--solid-works
Professional 3D CAD design and assembly of an Air Motor developed in SolidWorks, including individual components, assembly mates, assembly procedure, and final design visualization.

## 📌 Project Overview:

This project presents the complete 3D design and assembly of a
*Reciprocating Piston-Type Air Motor* developed using SOLIDWORKS.

The model demonstrates how compressed air can be converted into
mechanical rotary motion through a piston, connecting rod, crank,
crankshaft and flywheel mechanism.

The complete assembly was created by modelling individual mechanical
components and subsequently assembling them using appropriate
SOLIDWORKS mates.



## 🎯 Project Objectives:

The main objectives of this project are:

- To design an air motor using SOLIDWORKS.
- To model individual mechanical components in 3D.
- To understand piston-cylinder mechanisms.
- To understand the conversion of reciprocating motion into rotary motion.
- To assemble multiple components using SOLIDWORKS Assembly.
- To apply appropriate mechanical mates.
- To study the function of crank, connecting rod and flywheel.
- To develop practical CAD modelling and assembly skills.
- To document the complete engineering project professionally.

# ⚙️ Working Principle:

The air motor operates on the principle of converting the energy
of compressed air into mechanical rotary motion.

Compressed air is supplied to the cylinder and acts on the piston.
The pressure force causes the piston to move in a reciprocating
motion inside the cylinder.

The piston is connected to the connecting rod. The connecting rod
is connected to the crank mounted on the crankshaft.

When the piston moves, the connecting rod transfers the reciprocating
motion to the crank.

The crank converts this reciprocating motion into rotary motion of
the crankshaft.

The flywheel mounted on the crankshaft stores rotational energy and
helps maintain smoother and more uniform rotation.

### Energy Conversion:

Compressed Air
        ↓
Pressure Force
        ↓
Piston Reciprocating Motion
        ↓
Connecting Rod
        ↓
Crank Mechanism
        ↓
Crankshaft Rotation
        ↓
Flywheel
        ↓
Mechanical Rotary Output


# 🔄 Motion Conversion:

The most important mechanical principle demonstrated by this project
is:

*Reciprocating Motion → Rotary Motion*

The piston produces linear reciprocating motion.

The connecting rod transfers this motion to the crank.

The crank converts the reciprocating motion into rotary motion.

The crankshaft carries the rotary output, while the flywheel helps
smooth the rotational motion.


# 🧩 Component Functions:

## 1. Base Plate

The base plate provides a rigid foundation for the complete assembly.

It supports the motor structure and helps maintain the alignment of
the major components.


## 2. Support Bracket

The support bracket holds and supports the cylinder/crankshaft
assembly.

It also provides structural stability to the mechanism.


## 3. Cylinder

The cylinder provides the enclosed space in which the piston moves.

It guides the piston during reciprocating motion and forms the main
working chamber of the air motor.


## 4. Cylinder Head / Top Plate

The top plate closes the upper side of the cylinder assembly.

It also provides structural support and fastening locations.


## 5. Piston

The piston is the primary component that receives the force generated
by compressed air.

The piston moves back and forth inside the cylinder.


## 6. Connecting Rod

The connecting rod connects the piston to the crank.

Its main function is to transmit the reciprocating motion of the
piston to the crank mechanism.


## 7. Crank

The crank is mounted on the crankshaft and converts the linear
motion of the connecting rod into rotary motion.


## 8. Crankshaft

The crankshaft is the main rotating shaft of the mechanism.

It receives the motion from the crank and transfers rotary motion
to the flywheel and output.


## 9. Flywheel

The flywheel stores rotational kinetic energy.

It helps reduce fluctuations in rotational speed and provides
smoother rotation of the crankshaft.


## 10. Bearings

Bearings support the rotating shaft and reduce friction between
the shaft and stationary supports.


## 11. Fasteners

Bolts, screws and other fastening components secure the various
parts of the assembly.


# 🔧 Assembly Procedure:

The assembly was completed systematically in SOLIDWORKS.

### Step 01 – Base Plate

The base plate was inserted first and used as the primary reference
component.

The base plate was fixed in position.

### Step 02 – Support Brackets

The supporting brackets were positioned on the base plate.

Coincident and concentric mates were applied where required.

### Step 03 – Cylinder Assembly

The cylinder was positioned between the supporting structures.

The cylinder was aligned with the piston axis.

### Step 04 – Piston

The piston was inserted inside the cylinder.

The piston was aligned with the cylinder axis to allow reciprocating
motion.

### Step 05 – Crankshaft

The crankshaft was inserted through the supporting brackets.

Concentric mates were applied between the shaft and bearing/support
holes.

### Step 06 – Crank

The crank was mounted on the crankshaft.

The crank position was adjusted to provide the required mechanism
geometry.

### Step 07 – Connecting Rod

The connecting rod was connected between the piston and crank.

Pin/concentric relationships were used to establish the required
motion connection.

### Step 08 – Flywheel

The flywheel was mounted on the crankshaft.

The flywheel was aligned concentrically with the shaft.

### Step 09 – Cylinder Head / Top Plate

The top plate was positioned on the cylinder assembly and secured
using the required fasteners.

### Step 10 – Fasteners

Bolts and screws were added to secure the components to the base
and supporting structures.

### Step 11 – Final Assembly:

All components were checked for:

- Correct alignment
- Proper mating
- Interference
- Clearance
- Shaft rotation
- Piston movement
- Connecting rod movement
- Crank rotation
